# Prompt and Rubric Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to review synthetic prompts and public-safe rubric-writing
samples.

| Criterion | Weak | Acceptable | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prompt specificity | The task is vague or missing key constraints. | The task has a clear goal but limited constraints. | The task has a clear goal, audience, format, and constraints. |
| Explicit criteria | Requirements directly stated in the prompt are incomplete or mixed together. | Most explicit requirements are identified. | All important explicit requirements are separated and traceable to the prompt. |
| Implicit criteria | Implied expectations are missing or invented beyond the prompt. | Implied expectations are mostly reasonable. | Implied expectations are useful, bounded, and justified by the task context. |
| Atomicity | Criteria combine multiple unrelated judgments. | Some criteria are atomic. | Each criterion evaluates one clear quality dimension. |
| Self-contained wording | Criteria require hidden context to understand. | Criteria are mostly understandable. | Each criterion can be read and applied on its own. |
| Objective/subjective separation | Checkable requirements and judgment calls are mixed together. | Some distinction is present. | Objective checks and subjective quality judgments are clearly separated. |
| Failure modes | Failure modes are generic or absent. | Failure modes cover common mistakes. | Failure modes are concrete and tied to prompt constraints. |
| Confidentiality safety | Wording suggests private task origin or copied guidelines. | Public-safety note is present. | The sample is clearly synthetic, generalized, and free of private task material. |

Reviewer notes should cite the prompt constraint, identify whether the issue is
objective or subjective when relevant, and avoid copying private guideline
language.
